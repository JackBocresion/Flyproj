Scope

Findings

Open-source control projects (maturity snapshot)

  • Gaggiuino (Zer0-bit): “A Gaggia Classic control project using microcontrollers”; 2.7k stars, 387 forks, 2,380 commits; legacy STM32 BlackPill code on branch release/stm32-blackpill; the README page does not mention Gen 3 (GitHub, fetched 2026-11-10). Docs: targets Gaggia Classic family with 3-way valves, excluding Classic V2; Gen 1 Nano+Nextion, Gen 2 STM32+Nextion, Gen 3 STM32+ESP32; flow profiling absent on Gen 1, present on Gen 2/3 (docs site). Source status: the project site says Gen 3 is free with free updates but source is not available, under a non-commercial Creative Commons licence, per a Hackaday commenter quoting it (Hackaday 2022 comments). Aftermath reports Zer0-bit said the code stays private because of scalping and moved to an “open-core” model (Aftermath). A third-party review reports the public repo has no firmware on its default branch and last commit April 2025 (Synopolis). Verdict: source-available/closed for current generation, not open source in the OSI sense. I could not confirm the LICENSE text from the repo page itself.
  • GaggiMate (Jochen Niebuhr): ESP32, touch display, web UI, BLE/WebSocket integration surface, OTA with stable/beta/nightly channels (fork README via search). Upstream repo: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial), about 995 stars, 306 forks, 154 open issues, 1,122 commits; creator sells a kit at shop.gaggimate.eu; hardware design files moved to gaggimate/parts (GitHub, fetched 2026-11-10). A 2026 build write-up used a GaggiMate Pro with firmware v1.9.0 (Ulrich Lehner). The gaggimate org has a Home Assistant integration updated September 2026 (GitHub org). Kit price not found.
  • CleverCoffee (rancilio-pid): GPL-2.0, ESP32 only going forward, 418 stars, 189 forks, 1,416 commits, “active development for over 5 years”, v4.0.0 with browser web-flasher, official PCB, supports Rancilio Silvia, Gaggia, Quickmill; temperature PID (not pressure profiling) (GitHub). Genuinely open, but a temperature-control project.
  • Other PID projects: Perfect Coffee PID (ESP32), silvia-pi (Raspberry Pi), a Gaggia ESP32-C6 controller with HA/Grafana (search result). One ESP32 Silvia author states the stock v1 swings up to 20 C (kolkman).
  • Decent: DE1 machine firmware is closed and accepts only signed firmware (forum recollection of John Buckman, a UL-approval rationale claimed by a poster, unverified) (Home-Barista). Tablet app and API are described as open source by Decent (same thread). pyDE1: GPL-3.0, 81 stars, 377 commits, author uses it daily, needs Python 3.8+, bleak, MQTT 5 broker (GitHub). Decent Open Scale firmware GPLv3 (GitHub).
  • Meticulous: Kickstarter updates state APIs are open and documented, the server runs on the machine with no forced cloud, TypeScript and Python clients on GitHub (Kickstarter). Community: Home Assistant integration via zeroconf/Socket.IO (ha-meticulous), MeticAI, MCP server (MeticAI). Not confirmed: full firmware/schematics openness (only third-party marketing says so).
  • diyPresso: kit with open-source Arduino MKR WiFi 1010 control. One: EUR 1,250, 0.6 L custom copper boiler, 390x210x365 mm, 18 kg, no steam; Two: EUR 1,695 incl. VAT, 1300 W copper boiler + 1200 W thermoblock for steam, 21 kg, about 8-week lead time (diyPresso One, Two, observed 2026-11-10, EU). Firmware 1.8.0 (Jan 2026) adds Dynamic Feed Forward, vendor claims 60-80% better temperature stability (vendor claim, unverified) (downloads). Repo: GPL-3.0 code, 141 stars; no hardware design files visible; OTA on to-do (GitHub). Review: about 5 hours assembly (via search summary). Hackaday: “might not be the cheapest road” but economical for a proven design (Hackaday 2024).

From-scratch and heavily custom builds (all anecdote, forum-level evidence)

  • “Building a lever machine…. from scratch” (HB t41716, 36+ pages): builder converted a lathe to CNC over about a month while waiting for a foundry casting; prototyped group in aluminium machined from solid stock (final part under a quarter of stock volume); floating piston head to absorb axial misalignment in the first cast group body; worried about uncertified bar stock and lead (page 3, page 4). Cost view: a single unit is far too expensive; around ten units costs slightly more than a comparable reseller machine; time not counted (first page).
  • Open boiler lever: about EUR 280 parts, commenter says 70-80 hours (HB t40964). Another builder: about 120 hours and about EUR 1,300 materials, time untracked. A $350 all-aluminium build with cartridge heaters and PID, about 3 minute warm-up (via search summary of HB threads).
  • Open Source Lever Project (HB t39181): bought spring-lever group about $495, two Ascaso 1000 W thermoblocks about $25 each, 7075 aluminium plates, PID per block, thermoblock recovery about 60 s after a flush; goal was no machining and mostly off-the-shelf parts (thread). Dated (years old).
  • Modern DIY double boiler lever (HB t89560): 1500 W single 2 L boiler target with 1.5 bar steam, La San Marco group, 1200 W thermoblock preheating (page 3).
  • Four Bar Design: avoided a heavy high-ratio lever by using 12/16 g CO2 cartridges (about 2.6 g per pull, about $0.15 per shot), custom CNC stainless parts $102, author admits aversion to spending money “was almost certainly going to cost me more in time” (paraphrase) (post, 2020).
  • Other: Instructables lever machine from bar stock (link); Hackaday 2018 motorbike-parts machine and 2020 lever press (Hackaday).
  • Common lessons: plan several iterations; group head and boiler are the hardest and costliest parts to source; copper sheet is costly; time is under-counted; savings only at volume.

Mixed manual/automatic lever designs (relevant to the reader’s goal)

  • Modern hybrid levers: group heated separately, electronic flow control, pressure relief valve ends the shot, a lever movement or switch triggers flow, check valve holds water in the group (HB explainer). Spring-lever builds are noted as semi-manual: the user can assist the spring to shape the profile (HB).
  • Servo-assisted lever: only forum musings (raise, lower, stop hands-free) with debate on losing hands-on feel (HB first-lever thread). No documented, parts-listed build found. This is a gap and an opportunity.

Engineering fundamentals

  • Pumps: vibratory vs rotary: neither inherently makes better coffee; rotary quieter (about 55-60 dB claimed) and longer-lived, can be plumbed in; vibratory about 5-6 years home life; rotary usually uses a bypass, vibratory an OPV (Clive Coffee). A forum user found rotary pumps hard to flow-profile with a needle valve and favoured vibe or gear pump (single anecdote) (HB). Gear pump/brushless data not found.
  • Lead and materials: NSF/ANSI 372 limit 0.25% lead (2,500 ppm); NSF 51 allows brass up to that for coffee equipment; EDF petitioned to remove it (EDF, Dalla Corte). Daily Coffee News: NSF standard works on weighted share across parts, so a small leaded valve can pass (article). Forum: “lead-free” brass even in EU is “low lead” (HB). Electroless nickel opinions conflict.
  • Pressure vessel rules: UK Pressure Systems Safety Regulations 2000 apply to steam-making machines and treat fluid over 0.5 bar as a pressure system (Stratford DC leaflet); Virginia’s act covers hobby boilers (NDT, R-stamp welder) (VA code). Forum says espresso boilers are a “grey area” (HB). Retail safety valve 2.1 bar for home machines; most boilers run about 1-1.5 bar (EspressoParts). Advice: no brazed/oxy-acetylene boiler joints, use professional welding (forum). PED not found.
  • Electrical: earth as fault path, RCD/GFCI trips on element insulation failure, thermal fuse on elements; forum disagreement on GFCI value for all-metal machines (Papel Espresso, HB). No authoritative standard found.
  • Not found: group designs comparison (E61/saturated), PID tuning, transducers, flow meters, water chemistry. Rely on background knowledge, unsourced here.

Recommendation (my synthesis, not sourced evidence)

Modify-first. Take a machine with an accessible pump and valves, add an open controller, and put the unique work in a lever/servo-assist and motor-drive layer. Estimates from the anecdotes above: kit controller mods are weekend-scale (diyPresso kit about 5 h assembly); open-boiler lever from scratch 70-120 h and about EUR 280-1,300; a cast or machined spring group needs a workshop, a month of tooling, and multiple iterations. A spring-lever group bought as a part (about $495 in one thread) plus thermoblocks is the lowest-risk route to a “works by hand, can run automated” machine. Keep boiler pressure below the 0.5 bar regulatory threshold where possible (thermoblocks, or open/unpressurised boiler) to sidestep pressure-vessel issues. Licences: GaggiMate and Gaggiuino are non-commercial/closed; CleverCoffee (GPL-2.0), pyDE1 and diyPresso code (GPL-3.0) are open.

Gap 1: boiler vs thermoblock vs thermocoil

  • Heat-up (buying guides, rough): thermoblock about 3-60 s, thermocoil about 1-3 min, boilers 5-30 min depending on size and dual-boiler design (Craft Coffee Spot, Espresso Gadgets, Kairos). Figures disagree widely between sources.
  • Stability and steam: boilers act as a heat battery with the best stability and strongest, driest steam; thermoblock/thermocoil steam is wetter and weaker; a single boiler needs a wait to switch from brew to steam temperature (same guides). Opinion from sellers, not measured tests.
  • DIY thermoblock evidence (forum, anecdote): a back-of-envelope estimate that a 1000 W block with a roughly 6 ml path tops out near 3 ml/s while still reaching about 100 C; lever builders warn a thermoblock may not keep up with the burst of flow at shot start, so a fast PID or a restrictor may be needed; one build used a stepper-driven piston pump per thermoblock to set flow directly (HB dual thermoblock build). Flow-based feed-forward kept the temperature dip small in one open project (Hackaday.io OXIMITE); a basic thermocouple-and-relay loop already improved a stock thermostat a lot in another build.
  • Safety angle (inference): thermoblocks and thermocoils hold a few ml, so they are the easiest route to avoid a large stored-energy vessel; this is my reasoning, not a sourced rule. A steam boiler is where the hazard is.

Gap 2: transducers, flow meters, pumps (names and rough prices)

  • Pressure transducer: Peak Coffee lists a G1/4, 0-12 bar (1.2 MPa), 0.5-4.5 V, 304 stainless sensor at $38 (US shop, cached) (Peak Coffee); the same store showed a Gaggiuino GEN2 complete kit at $278 and a pressure tap block at $48 (category page). An older HB thread reports a used Huba 501 bought for about $80 and a Digmesa flow sensor on the pump’s low-pressure side (HB, thread several years old).
  • Flow meter: Digmesa turbine meters appear at roughly 8-78 GBP on TME depending on model (TME Digmesa); espresso-specific Digmesa units cost more (about 229 AUD, out of stock) (Coffee Parts AU); Espressoparts lists an NSF-approved Digmesa 0.033-0.40 L/min meter (Espressoparts). Whether these match Gaggiuino or GaggiMate inputs is not confirmed.
  • Gear/brushless pumps: Fluid-o-Tech FG200-300-400 series is a 316L pump-motor unit with PEEK/PTFE gears, 24 V brushless drive with speed control, 30-210 l/h, up to 12 bar differential (FG309: 40-190 l/h, 9 bar max) (Fluid-o-Tech, primary). A dealer lists a 24 V external gear pump at $1,808 (Espressoparts); used Fluid-o-Tech pumps on eBay run roughly $100-300 (search-summary, unverified).
  • Profiling practice (HB, 4-6 years old): builders vary pump speed and read a transducer with an Arduino; speed-to-pressure is non-linear because of internal slip; with a bypass valve, speed mainly sets ramp rate; a Fluid-o-Tech contact warned the FG100 with 9 mm gears barely reaches 9 bar from atmospheric inlet, so inlet pressure helps; the FG204 gear set uses a flat Teflon seal that can leak, FG304 uses O-rings (TMFR thread, MGFR test thread). Sensor noise: a La Marzocco GS/3 profiling project saw unstable pressure and jumpy flow readings, possibly missed Arduino interrupts (HB).
  • Relevance: a motor-driven gear pump with speed control is the natural actuator for a hybrid machine, and a separate spring/lever stage is not required for it.

Gap 3: OPV, pressurestat, safety valve, pressure-vessel rules

  • OPV: spring valve opening near 9 bar (9-9.5 bar common on Breville per a forum), returns water to the tank; a 9 bar Lelit OPV is a retail part (HB, Jet Black).
  • Boiler safety valve: Espressoparts sells a 2.1 bar (about 30 psi) valve for home single boilers and says it replaces a 1.8 bar part; it warns against fitting a lower-rated valve or bypassing it (Espressoparts). Forum values range from about 1.6 to 3 bar and conflict. Pressurestat: steam boilers typically held near 1-1.5 bar; a stuck pressurestat is the usual cause of a valve lifting; adjustment is fine (about 0.1 bar per six half-turns of the screw, per La Valentina guide). A reported failure: Pasquini Livia 90 blew up, pressure keeps rising (thread title only, details not read).
  • SCA: regulations generally require relief valves to be factory-set and marked with the activation pressure (SCA article). Use certified, marked valves; never lower or bypass.
  • Rules: EU PED 2014/68/EU applies to stationary equipment above 0.5 bar; smaller items fall to “sound engineering practice” (PED guidelines). UK Safed guidance treats cafe boilers as minor pressure systems (Safed PSG08). Iowa exempts electric boilers of six gallons or less that are integral to an espresso machine (Iowa code 89.4). The PS x V category limits, and how a one-off home-built boiler is treated, were not found. EN 14222 is a stainless steam boiler standard for sterilizers, not espresso machines (NBN). No hydrotest rule found.
  • Practical read (inference): the lowest-risk design keeps pressure under 0.5 bar or uses thermoblocks; if a steam boiler is built, use certified valve and pressurestat, professional welding or a bought boiler, and check local rules.

Gap 5: E61 vs saturated vs lever

  • Retailer comparisons: saturated group is bolted to the boiler, gives tight PID control and faster warm-up (about 5-10 min); E61 uses a thermosyphon loop and heavy brass mass, passive stability, 20-45 min warm-up, gasket upkeep, widely available spares (Complete Home Barista, Coffeeionado, Kairos). Sources disagree on warm-up and on spares availability.
  • For a DIY: E61 needs a bought group and a boiler with a thermosyphon loop; saturated needs a custom boiler flange (the harder fabrication). Neither supports a hand-lever stroke. No lever-group thermal-stability data found; the earlier ~$495 spring group plus thermoblocks remains the documented hybrid route.

Gap 6: lead and food-safe materials

  • CW617N is a leaded forging brass; “lead-free CW617N” does not exist, lead-free brass is a separate alloy family (silicon/bismuth) (IFAN, vendor).
  • “Lead-free” often means under 0.25% lead; stagnant water can still pick up lead; La Marzocco reportedly moved to steel for groups and boilers (Barista Hustle).
  • Example: Lelit Elizabeth vendor page lists a CW510L boiler top (0.2% max lead) with a chrome-treated lower section, and CW617N nickel-plated fittings and valves (Comiso). HB threads include a user-measured lead report (HB) and coating-damage worries.
  • Practical guidance (partly my inference): choose 316 stainless or potable-rated (NSF/ANSI 61/372) parts for water contact, keep brass for non-wetted parts, and flush new brass. No authoritative espresso-specific rule found.
