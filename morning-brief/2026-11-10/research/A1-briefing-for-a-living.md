## Findings

### Intelligence: estimative language
- Kent's 1964 essay "Words of Estimative Probability" (Studies in Intelligence, Fall 1964) mapped words to odds. Midpoints reported consistently: almost certain 93% (±6), probable 75% (±12), chances about even 50% (±10), probably not 30% (±10), almost certainly not 7% (±5) ([GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/intell/ops/probability.htm); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Words_of_estimative_probability)). Opinion/history, not a trial: these were Kent's ranges, based on colleagues' readings.
- Origin story: NIE 29-51 (March 1951), "Probability of an Invasion of Yugoslavia in 1951", said an attack was a "serious possibility". Kent says he meant about 65:35 for an attack; colleagues read it as much lower, with reported spreads of ~20-80% (secondary, unconfirmed against primary) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Words_of_estimative_probability); [Kesselman thesis](https://www.files.ethz.ch/isn/55739/kesselman_thesis_final.pdf)).
- Kent split colleagues into "poets" (prefer words) and "mathematicians" (prefer numbers) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Words_of_estimative_probability)).
- Modern practice: ICD 203 prescribes seven likelihood bands (e.g. almost no chance/remote 01-05%, very unlikely 05-20%, unlikely 20-45%, roughly even 45-55%, likely 55-80%, almost certain 95-99%) and forbids combining a confidence level and a likelihood term in one sentence ([ICD 203 PDF](https://www.intelligence.gov/assets/documents/intelligence-community-directives/ICD_203.pdf); table as reproduced in [GitHub copy](https://github.com/wesinator/ICD203-intel-analysis)). Table values come from a secondary reproduction; check against the PDF.
- Evidence on perception: a 2023 survey of 123 people found perceptions of these words largely consistent with Kent's era, with wide spreads ([arXiv 2405.15185](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15185) via search summary). Wording remains understood differently by different readers, so a numeric range is safer.
- Evidence on accuracy: Mandel and Barnes (PNAS 2014) assessed 1,514 strategic intelligence forecasts from a Canadian unit (2005-2011); discrimination and calibration were "very good", better for senior analysts and easier questions; analysts were more underconfident on high-importance questions ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/4121776/); [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2014-07-accuracy-strategic-intelligence.html)).

### Intelligence: PDB format
- BLUF structure: the what, the why, what comes next; typically one page ([Cipher Brief, Becky Root](https://www.thecipherbrief.com/column_article/what-i-learned-writing-for-the-presidents-daily-brief)). Early PDBs were very short: Brennan said the first issue was 14 items, most no more than two sentences ([CIA, Brennan keynote](https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/brennan-delivers-keynote-at-presidents-daily-brief-public-release-event)).
- Live briefer: Priess (former daily briefer under Clinton and Bush) argues the briefer's value is answering questions and discussing nuance, not late updates (reported in a search summary; exact wording could not be confirmed) ([Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/getting-it-right-first-customer); [Stratfor](https://worldview.stratfor.com/article/presidents-book-secrets-david-priess)). Context only: Bush reportedly valued candid "I don't know; I'll get it for you" answers from briefers (secondary).
- Meador and Cerf (Studies in Intelligence, Dec 2013) proposed a tablet PDB with the briefer "driving" interactive content ([CIA](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/studies-in-intelligence/volume-57-no-4/rethinking-the-presidents-daily-intelligence-brief); [Nextgov](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2014/03/a-wish-list-for-a-digitized-presidents-daily-brief/255007/)). Proposal, not evidence of effect.
- Helgerson, Getting to Know the President (CSI 1996): covers briefings of candidates and presidents-elect, based on declassified records and interviews; as Clinton's briefer he reportedly avoided trying to influence Clinton's views. No findings retrieved ([CSI/GovInfo listing](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/GOVPUB-PREX3-PURL-gpo31699)).

### Intelligence: critiques and failures
- Tyranny of current intelligence: a CIA analytic-pathologies report has a section so titled, calling it "a plague recognized by all informed critics" per the search summary ([CIA PDF](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/e228eea610f76862a05274ca88500679/analytic_pathologies_report.pdf)). A 2016 Air University paper and a 2010 Intelligence and National Security article make the same claim for the US and UK ([JCS paper](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/Education/jpme_papers/rojas_t.pdf?ver=2017-12-29-142154-987); [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02684527.2010.537118)). Opinion/analysis, not measurement.
- August 6 2001 PDB: titled "Bin Ladin Determined to Strike in US"; prepared in response to the president's questions; per the Commission, the 36th PDB item that year on Bin Ladin/al Qaeda and the first devoted to a US attack. The White House fact sheet says it was mostly historical analysis, with no mention of planes as weapons ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bin_Ladin_Determined_To_Strike_in_US); [White House fact sheet](https://irp.fas.org/news/2004/04/wh041004.html); [Commission ch. 8](https://9-11commission.gov/report/911Report_Ch8.htm)). The chapter is titled "The System Was Blinking Red", from Tenet. The lesson drawn depends on who reads it: a historical item delivered into a flood of 36 items, versus a missed alarm. Contested.
- Politicization/pressure: Morell, a briefer to Bush, is reported to call Libby's interventions "the most blatant attempt to politicize intelligence that I saw in thirty three years" (reviewer's paraphrase/quote, unverified), yet in a 2015 NPR interview called the pressure-on-Iraq thesis "a myth" ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2015/05/10/405624427/flawed-intelligence-in-the-war-of-our-time)). The 2005 WMD Commission said tough policymaker questioning is not politicization ([report](https://policy.defense.gov/portals/11/Documents/hdasa/references/GPO-WMD.pdf)). Kerr's 2004 review warned of analysts giving more certainty than the facts supported (secondary, GMU chapter).
- Morell put his confidence in the bin Laden compound at about 60% ([Army Times/AP](https://www.armytimes.com/news/pentagon-congress/2020/07/03/only-verified-intelligence-a-look-at-presidents-briefings/)). Illustrates stating confidence to the reader.

### Medicine: morning report
- Multicentre survey: residents report high attendance, clinical duties are the main barrier (85%); residents rate clinical reasoning (82%) and evidence-based medicine (77%) as top topics ([JGIM 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-020-06351-7)). Program director presence linked to higher attendance (78% vs 61%) ([PMC8477724](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8477724/)). Older survey: most programs lack written goals and 77% do not track outcomes ([Validation review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259631906_Morning_Report_Can_an_Established_Medical_Education_Tradition_Be_Validated)). A resident-as-teacher intervention improved meeting audience needs, d=0.26 ([PMC1435882](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1435882/)). Evidence is perception surveys; no patient-outcome data found.

### Medicine: handoffs
- I-PASS (Starmer et al., NEJM 2014): nine hospitals, 10,740 admissions; medical errors 24.5 to 18.8 per 100 admissions (23% relative reduction); preventable adverse events 4.7 to 3.3 (30%); nonpreventable events unchanged; oral handoff time unchanged, 2.4 vs 2.5 min ([NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa1405556); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/11/141105203533.htm)). Design: prospective pre-post, no randomization or concurrent control, authors state it precludes establishing causation; reductions seen at six of nine sites ([MHS IV review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK613742/)). A PSNet page mislabels 23% as preventable adverse events.
- Joint Commission: "about 80%" of serious errors involving miscommunication at handoff traces to one spokesperson; other estimates 50-80% or over 75%. In its Hand-off Communications Project receivers judged about 37% of handoffs unsuccessful and full implementers cut defective handoffs by 52% on average ([Healthcare IT News](https://www.healthcareitnews.com/news/joint-commission-confronts-deadly-miscommunications); [Sentinel Event Alert 58](https://jointcommission.org/-/media/tjc/documents/resources/patient-safety-topics/sentinel-event/sea_58_hand_off_comms_9_6_17_final_(1).pd)). Treat 80% as folklore.

### Medicine: surgical checklists and briefings
- Haynes et al. (NEJM 2009): 8 hospitals in 8 countries, 7,688 patients (3,733 before, 3,955 after); death 1.5% to 0.8% (P=0.003); inpatient complications 11.0% to 7.0% (P<0.001); 19-item checklist; before/after design ([NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa0810119); [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2009/01/surgical-safety-checklist-drops-deaths-and-complications-by-more-than-one-third/)).
- Urbach et al. (NEJM 2014), Ontario: 101 hospitals, 109,341 procedures before and 106,370 after; adjusted mortality 0.71% to 0.65% (OR 0.91, P=0.13); no significant complication change ([NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa1308261); [ICES](https://www.ices.on.ca/news-releases/study-shows-introduction-of-surgical-safety-checklists-in-ontario-have-not-reduced-post-operative-mortality-or-complications/)). Compliance was not measured. Critics (including Gawande) say a mandate without training, culture work or compliance monitoring explains the null; a blogger calculated about 40% power (not Gawande's) ([Incidental Economist](https://theincidentaleconomist.com/wordpress/when-checklists-work-and-when-they-dont/); [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/821910); [KevinMD](https://kevinmd.com/2014/03/nejm-publish-bad-study-checklists.html)).
- Reames et al. 2015, Michigan Medicare: difference-in-differences against 950 non-participating hospitals; 30-day mortality RR 1.03 (0.97-1.10); $516 higher index-admission payments ([Medical Care abstract](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/lww-medicalcare/abstract/10.1097/mlr.0000000000000277~evaluation-of-the-effectiveness-of-a-surgical-checklist-in?redirectionsource=fulltextview)). Second null result in a real-world rollout.
- Lingard et al. (Arch Surg 2008): 302 procedures; checklist-structured team briefing reduced communication failures per procedure (one source: three-fold) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18209148/); [PSNet](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/evaluation-preoperative-checklist-and-team-briefing-among-surgeons-nurses-and)). An earlier paper reports paradoxical effects of briefings on OR performance ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10111-007-0086-8)).
- Gawande's team-introduction step: reported as having felt "hokey" to him, justified by research that people who know names work together better (HuffPost review, secondary) ([HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/check-please-atul-gawande_b_410507)). The WHO form says it "is not intended to be comprehensive" ([WHO form](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/patient-safety/9789241598590-eng-checklist.pdf)). Pronovost central-line checklist: Keystone linked to about 10% fewer deaths, which researchers say they could not attribute definitively ([JHU Gazette](https://gazette.jhu.edu/2011/02/14/safety-checklist-use-yields-10-percent-drop-in-hospital-deaths)).

### Military briefings
- Bumiller, NYT 27 Apr 2010 "We Have Met the Enemy and He Is PowerPoint": Gen. Mattis said "PowerPoint makes us stupid"; McMaster (banned PowerPoint at Tal Afar 2005) said it can create "the illusion of understanding and the illusion of control" and that "some problems in the world are not bullet-izable". The article also describes McChrystal reacting to the "spaghetti" COIN-dynamics slide. Some sites misattribute the Mattis line to McMaster ([article PDF repost](http://www.californiaskywatch.com/warcosts/wp-content/uploads/war-cost/472C_2010_WAR_We_Have_Met_the_Enemy_He_Is_Powerpoint_by_Bumiller_NYTimes_April_27_2010.pdf); [Nextgov](https://www.nextgov.com/people/2010/09/speaking-truth-to-powerpoint/223359/)). Critique is anecdotal; no outcome data.

### Aviation CRM
- CRM training started in 1979 after high-profile accidents. Meta-analyses find effects mostly at attitude and learning level; about half of studies in one 2006 review mixed; a healthcare umbrella review found outcome evidence unsupported ([O'Connor meta-analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/247502783_Crew_Resource_Management_Training_Effectiveness_A_Meta-Analysis_and_Some_Critical_Needs); [Umbrella review](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/journalpatientsafety/fulltext/10.1097/pts.0000000000000816~what-do-we-really-know-about-crew-resource-management-in)). Briefing-specific evidence not found.

### Trading desks
- No usable evidence found on morning meetings or research morning notes. Only generic: buy-side reports are typically one to two pages ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buy-side_analyst)).

## Disagreements, failure modes and caveats
- Strong designs are rare. I-PASS and Haynes are before/after; Ontario and Michigan, run as real rollouts, were null. The best defensible summary: structured communication helps when implemented with training and measured compliance; mandated or ritual use may not.
- The Ontario null is itself contested (power, 3-month windows, no compliance data). Gawande's rebuttal is an inference.
- I-PASS did not lengthen the oral handoff (2.4 vs 2.5 min), a useful design lesson: structure without more time.
- Words vs numbers: Kent's table versus ICD 203 bands differ in numbers; readers vary. Kent himself used a numeric "65:35" private reading for "serious possibility" versus colleagues' much lower readings.
- Format versus content: the PDB 36-items-before-Aug-6 story cuts both ways: volume of items can numb readers; a historical piece may fail to stand out.
- Politicization: a briefer's own accounts conflict (Morell on Libby versus "a myth"). Do not treat a single memoir line as the consensus.
- The "80% of errors" handoff figure is a single spokesperson's estimate.
- CRM evidence is mostly attitudes and training, not briefing formats.
- PowerPoint critique is quote-driven; no measurement of decision quality.
